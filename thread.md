## 1/7

My AI agent moves real money on-chain. It signs transactions, claims NFTs, and lists them for sale — while I sleep. So here's the uncomfortable question: how do I actually know it did what it said it did? 🧵 #AgentGames @ZKVProtocol

## 2/7

An agent's word isn't evidence. It can tell me 'minted, listed, claimed' — and I only find out the truth when I check the chain myself. Logs can be edited. Screenshots can be faked. A chat transcript is not a receipt.

## 3/7

I learned this the hard way. My agent once auto-claimed a prize with no fresh confirmation from me. It worked — but it also proved the point: if it can act without me noticing, it can also be wrong without me noticing.

## 4/7

The fix isn't more trust. It's proof. A zero-knowledge proof lets an agent show 'I did exactly this, and the chain confirms it' — without revealing the private details behind it. Verify, don't trust.

## 5/7

That's what zkVerify is for: a dedicated layer that verifies ZK proofs, so every app doesn't have to build its own setup. My agent's claim becomes a checkable fact, not a story.

## 6/7

Where this actually matters: agents that trade money, approve loans, sign contracts, or moderate content. Anywhere 'just trust me' isn't good enough — and in crypto, it never is.
